About Tarot Cards

Introduction to Tarot

Tarot cards are a deck of 78 cards used for divination, self-reflection, and spiritual guidance. The most widely known tarot deck is the Rider-Waite-Smith (RWS) Tarot, first published in 1909.

The RWS Tarot was created by Arthur Edward Waite and illustrated by Pamela Colman Smith. What made this deck revolutionary was that it included detailed symbolic imagery on all cards, not just the major cards as was common in earlier decks.

Structure of the Tarot

The tarot deck consists of 78 cards divided into two main sections:

Major Arcana

22 cards (from The Fool to The World) representing major life themes and spiritual journeys.

Minor Arcana

56 cards divided into four suits: Wands (creativity/fire), Cups (emotions/water), Swords (intellect/air), and Pentacles (material/earth).

Symbolism in Tarot

Tarot cards are rich with symbolism drawn from various traditions including mythology, astrology, numerology, and religious iconography. Each card contains multiple layers of meaning that can be interpreted in different ways depending on the context.

The imagery in tarot cards often speaks to the subconscious mind, helping to reveal insights that might not be immediately apparent through conscious thought alone.

How Tarot Is Used

While many associate tarot with fortune-telling, it's more accurately described as a tool for self-reflection and gaining perspective. Tarot readings can help clarify situations, reveal unseen influences, and suggest possible outcomes based on current energies.

Modern tarot practice often focuses on psychological insight and personal growth rather than predicting specific future events. The cards provide a framework for examining our thoughts, feelings, and behaviors from new angles.
